Clinic rollups and physician consolidation, by the numbers

Healthcare private equity deal value, physician-practice transaction counts and multiples, ownership shifts, specialty rollup evidence, and the antitrust record, from published sources.

Three different kinds of number sit behind this name, and they must not be mixed. The first kind measures deal flow: how much capital sponsors deploy into healthcare, how many physician practices change hands, and at what multiples. The second kind measures the seller base: how many physicians still own their practices, and how fast that share is falling. The third kind measures the limit: where consolidation produced a dominant local position, and what regulators did about it. The first kind tells a reader the market is active. The second tells a reader why it stays active. The third tells a reader where it stops.

What the latest numbers show is a market that has come back to record scale after a two-year slowdown, fed by a seller base that keeps shrinking: 2025 healthcare private equity deal value set a record at $191 billion, provider-services deals jumped 57%, and exit value nearly tripled from 2024 — while the share of physicians in private practice fell to 42.2%, and more than four in five physicians now work for hospitals or corporate entities.

How to read these numbers

Included: Bain's 2025 healthcare private equity totals; Irvin Levin's announced physician-practice deal counts; Scope Research's 33-deal multiple sample and FOCUS's dental multiple ranges, both labeled as samples and ranges rather than market averages; AMA 2024 ownership shares; PAI-Avalere 2018–2026 employment and acquisition totals; Health Affairs' 2012–2021 growth estimate; the radiology and dental specialty counts; and the FTC's USAP allegations, labeled as allegations. Excluded: any single count of "clinic rollup platforms" as a category — no published source counts platforms that way, so none is presented here; any whole-market rollup price index, which does not exist in published form; and undated practitioner claims presented as dated figures.

Multiples from announced-deal samples skew toward larger transactions and are labeled as such wherever they appear. Ownership shares from different surveys are never averaged: the AMA survey and the PAI-Avalere study use different methods and populations, and their figures are reported side by side, not combined. Forecasts and planning targets are labeled as forecasts and targets, not actuals.
